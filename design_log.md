# Design log

One or two lines per part: what you tried, what failed, what you changed. Include at least one failure.

- Part 0: Recorded the words, sentences and room-noise blocks using the personal read-aloud code. Ran the splitting and audit workflow and retained the original recordings, clips and audit outputs.
- Part 1: Implemented MFCC extraction from first principles using NumPy and SciPy. Initially, the Mel filterbank scaling caused differences from Librosa. Corrected the Mel frequency conversion and filterbank implementation, achieving close numerical agreement with Librosa. Implemented autocorrelation-based F0 estimation and compared the results with Librosa pYIN. Refined pitch selection using normalization and continuity checks, then saved the comparison plots and results.
- Part 2: Implemented diagonal Gaussian log-emission probabilities, Forward and Viterbi algorithms in log space, and segmental K-means training for ten word-specific three-state HMMs. Trained each model for five iterations using the training repetitions while reserving repetition 1 for testing. Evaluated the ten held-out recordings and obtained 90% recognition accuracy (9 out of 10 correct). The word think was misclassified as sink. Generated and saved the confusion matrix.
- Part 3: Implemented the CTC forward algorithm from first principles using NumPy and log-space dynamic programming. Expanded the target sequence with blank labels and implemented valid state transitions, including the repeated-label restriction. Verified the calculated toy CTC probability against PyTorch CTCLoss. NumPy produced 0.3073 and PyTorch produced approximately 0.30729994, with an absolute difference of approximately 5.82e-08.
- Part 4: Implemented add-one and interpolated Kneser-Ney models, evaluated Brown corpus perplexity, integrated CMUdict and G2P fallback, and recorded three weighted personal pronunciation alternatives.
- Part 5: Built the toy lexicon with 10 states. Hand determinisation combines common input prefixes into 7 states. Minimisation merges the three equivalent terminal states, leaving 5 states. The three original phone-to-word mappings and their costs are preserved. The notebook implementation of WFST composition and own-sentence L o G decoding still requires a clean-kernel rerun before these operations can be treated as fully verified.
- Part 6: Precomputed wav2vec2 posteriors and Whisper transcripts, implemented WER and CTC prefix beam search with language-model fusion, and selected alpha = 0.0 (all tested alpha values produced the same tuning WER) using the prescribed tuning sentences. Evaluated held-out sentences at beam widths 1 and 8 and measured WER under clean, 10 dB and 0 dB conditions; the aggregate noise WER increased as SNR decreased.
- Part 7: Implemented and ran the text-to-speech and Griffin–Lim workflow, saved the reconstructed audio, recorded the 30-iteration spectral convergence value (0.1370159), and evaluated STOI at 10 dB and 0 dB. Generated the convergence and STOI figures from the saved outputs.
